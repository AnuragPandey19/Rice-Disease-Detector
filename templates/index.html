📸 How to photograph the leaf

✓
Do
  • One leaf, laid flat on plain paper
  • Leaf fills most of the frame
  • Even, indirect light
✕
Avoid
  • The leaf small or far away in the frame
  • Hard shadows, glare, or motion blur
  • Backgrounds unrelated to a paddy field

Field photographs work — the model was retrained on real paddy-field images. Accuracy is still higher on a leaf laid flat on plain paper (96.5%) than on a field photo (81.7%), so use plain paper when you can. If a photo is rejected, the crop tool usually fixes it.

          About

          Built, broken, and rebuilt

          A two-stage ensemble of five deep-learning models classifies rice leaf images into seven categories: healthy, not-a-rice-leaf, and five common diseases — one bacterial (bacterial leaf blight) and four fungal (brown spot, leaf blast, leaf scald, narrow brown spot).

          The first version scored 96.68% and was wrong about why. It had learned that a leaf on plain paper meant “rice” and a busy background meant “not rice” — so real field photographs were rejected outright. Both stages were retrained on a rebuilt dataset with hard negatives and 800 real paddy-field images.

          Scope and limits. It is a triage aid, not a substitute for an agronomist. Leaf scald and narrow brown spot remain studio-only in the training data, so treat those two results with extra caution. It does not cover nutrient deficiencies, pest damage, or the other 15-plus rice diseases, and it cannot say “something else”.
